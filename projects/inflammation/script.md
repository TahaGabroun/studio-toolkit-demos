# Acute inflammation, in focus

Promise: Explain vascular changes, neutrophil recruitment and resolution using anatomically identified structures.

## scene-1 — 0.400–8.546s

Local injury activates resident cells. Their inflammatory mediators signal nearby vessels and recruit immune defenses.

[VISUAL: Identify a local injury above the vessel; dim the surrounding tissue.]

## scene-2 — 9.046–17.043s

Arterioles dilate, increasing local blood flow. This contributes to the redness and warmth of acute inflammation.

[VISUAL: Focus the arteriolar inflow and widen its lumen without calling a capillary an arteriole.]

## scene-3 — 17.543–26.794s

Increased permeability, especially in postcapillary venules, lets fluid and plasma proteins enter tissue, contributing to swelling.

[VISUAL: Identify postcapillary venule; show protein-rich fluid crossing into tissue.]

## scene-4 — 27.294–34.707s

Neutrophils roll, adhere, and cross the venular wall. They then follow chemical signals toward the injury.

[VISUAL: Track a segmented-nucleus neutrophil through rolling, adhesion and transmigration.]

## scene-5 — 35.207–44.708s

When the trigger is controlled, active resolution limits recruitment and clears debris. These overlapping processes are compressed here for teaching.

[VISUAL: Fade to a restrained repair state; label teaching-time compression, not a literal biological clock.]
